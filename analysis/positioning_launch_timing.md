# LUMEN positioning and launch timing

## Target and positioning

The clearest initial target is **Urban Wellness Professionals**: the segment is naturally aligned with clean ingredients and the adaptogen benefit, and its quotes support willingness to pay for a credible functional product. Fitness & Gym-Goers are a strong secondary audience because they compare LUMEN directly with VoltFit and demand proof of taste and performance.

LUMEN should position between premium performance and boutique adaptogenic brands: a clean-label, everyday functional beverage with a credible natural-caffeine story, priced at the balanced €2.19 base case rather than trying to be the cheapest option.

## Competitive frame

| Competitor | Positioning | Indicative single-can range |
|---|---|---:|
| PulsUp | Mass market | €1.07–€1.28 |
| Mate Libre | Heritage / loyal niche | €1.59–€1.83 |
| VoltFit | Premium performance | €2.37–€2.72 |
| Root & Rise | Boutique adaptogenic | €2.98–€3.11 |

At €2.19, LUMEN can communicate premium cues while remaining below the established premium competitors. The deliberate trade-off is less immediate price-led reach than PulsUp and less extreme premium signaling than Root & Rise.

## City sequencing

Berlin is the strongest first-city hypothesis because it has the largest illustrative regional market share (18%) and a 9% regional CAGR assumption. Munich is the logical second pilot (15% share and 9% CAGR). Hamburg, Cologne, and Frankfurt should follow once the team has evidence on repeat purchase and channel economics.

These regional splits are illustrative planning assumptions, not observed LUMEN sales.

## Timing

The seasonality index peaks in July (138), followed by August (128) and June (132). A practical launch window is late spring to early summer, with May used for distribution setup and sampling, followed by a June–July demand test. The trade-off is higher operational pressure during the peak season; a smaller May soft launch can reduce execution risk.

## Qualitative / quantitative reconciliation

- Wellness professionals support the clean-ingredient and adaptogen story, but taste is a conversion risk.
- Fitness users want performance and taste proof, not packaging alone.
- Commuters require physical availability near stations, so they are better treated as a later reach channel than the first strategic positioning anchor.
- Students are price-sensitive and should be addressed through promotions or packs rather than the core premium proposition.

## What this PR does not claim

There is no German historical sales series. City, segment, and timing choices are hypotheses to test using the cockpit and pilot results, not forecasts presented as facts.
